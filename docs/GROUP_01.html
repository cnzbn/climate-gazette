How to read the E3CI

  • For every month, place and component (heat, cold, drought, rain, wind, hail, fire), the E3CI compares that month with the same month in 1981–2010: 0 = a normal month, 1 = one standard deviation above normal.
  • Values above 1 are extreme, in three classes: 1–2, 2–3, above 3 (the darker the colour, the more extreme).
  • Because every month is compared with itself, an extreme in winter or autumn means unusual for that season. For example, a heat E3CI of 1.2 in July and of 2.5 in January does not mean that January was hotter than July. It means that January was much further from what is usual for January than July was from what is usual for July.

Data: E3CI – European Extreme Events Climate Index, from the Copernicus ERA5 reanalysis.

When in the year?

Comparison of two periods: 1981-2010 vs 2011-2026, divided by seasons.

How to read this chart

A rose for each weather event and period: * every petal is a calendar month: January at the top, then clockwise (April on the right, July at the bottom, October on the left); * the area of a petal, not its length, is proportional to the share of country-months that were extreme in that month, as in Nightingale’s roses; * inside each petal the classes go from the centre outwards: 1–2 (the lightest shade), 2–3, above 3 (the darkest); * top row: the first period, bottom row: the second one (chosen in PERIODS).

Remember: every month is compared with the same month of 1981–2010, so an extreme in winter is unusual for winter.

Compare countries in Europe: who is hit the hardest over the years?

How to read this chart

Rows are countries (or regions) and columns are years. Each cell shows the E3CI class of the selected month. With MONTH = 0 it shows the class of the year’s highest monthly value. The bar on the right counts the years in which that value was above 1. With SORT_BY = “count”, the countries with the most extreme years are at the top.

One map per year: Europe trends at a glance

How to read this chart

There is one map for each year, all for the same component and the same calendar month. Each country is coloured by its E3CI class, and countries outside the selection are light grey. Read the maps left to right and top to bottom, like a comic strip.

The barcode of extremes

How to read this chart

Rows are the seven components and columns are years. A cell’s shade counts the months in that year above THRESHOLD: 0, 1–2, 3–5, or 6 or more. Each row uses its own component colour. The last year is incomplete, so its cell is not directly comparable with the others.

Comparison between two extreme weather components

How to read this chart

Rows are the TOP_N areas with the most months above THRESHOLD, counting both components together. Each dot is one month above the threshold. Its colour shows the component and its size is proportional to the E3CI value.

Month by month, and the records

How to read this chart

Each bar is one month. Bars above the dashed line at 1 are extreme and coloured by class. Bars below 1 are grey, and negative values mean the month was below its usual level. The dots mark the TOP_N most intense months, which are also listed on the right.

Let’s explore a specific extreme weather event closer

How to read this chart

The chart has three panels:

  • Top: time series with every month since 1981, with the event highlighted and its value and rank among all months.
  • Bottom left: the same calendar month in every year, with the event’s rank among those months.
  • Bottom right: the 12 regions in Europe with the highest values in that month. If the selected area is not among them, it is added as the last row.

Together they show how unusual the month was locally and how widespread the event was.
